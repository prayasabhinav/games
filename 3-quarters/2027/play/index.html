3Quarters

Three pieces of one pattern, all facing the same corner, make three quarters of a circle. The first to make a circle in each of the four patterns wins.

    The rules
    1. Set up. Play on the whole board, whatever the number of players. Each player takes their eight quarter-circle pieces. Toss a coin to see who starts.
    2. Pieces. A piece fills one square and faces one of its four corners. Its pattern sets its points: obtuse 1, median 2, two-point 3, acute 4 (in this test version, the patterns and their values are proposals).
    3. A turn. Roll the die (d4) first. Then arrange one piece: add one of yours to an empty square, turn any piece on the board, yours or another player's, or remove one. A piece you add or turn goes round as many corners as the die shows; a 4 turns it full circle.
    4. Three quarters. Three pieces in three of the four squares around one shared corner, each facing that corner, make a 3/4 circle. All three must have the same pattern. They can belong to anyone.
    5. Remove. To remove a quadrant, place a quadrant with the same pattern on it. Take both (without adding the points that both pieces have). A player can remove a piece only three times. When nobody has a remove left, each player gets one back.
    6. Score. The player who completes the 3/4 circle removes the pieces from the board and takes as many points as the constituent pieces. If one of its quadrants is on a yellow square, the score is doubled.
    7. Keep the board. Avoid emptying the board: the player whose move empties it loses 10 points.
    8. Time. Each move has to be made in 20 s. Use a mobile to keep time.
    9. Win. The first player to complete a circle in each of the four patterns wins. The scoreboard shows which patterns each player has made.
    10. Start again. If every player still needs a pattern that cannot make a circle as things stand (no corner can gather three of it, and nobody holding a piece of it has a remove left), every piece on the board goes back to its owner and play goes on.
    11. Quiet round. A whole round in which every player only turned a piece and nobody made a circle. If your turn completes one, and you could have added or removed a piece instead, the game ends and you cannot win. The highest score among the other players wins. If turning was your only move, play goes on. A turn lost to the timer counts as turning a piece, except while the board is empty.
    How this version reads the rules
    1. A new piece is set down as the pieces are printed, facing the lower-right corner of its square. It then goes round clockwise as many corners as the die shows: 1 faces lower-left, 2 upper-left, 3 upper-right, and 4 brings it full circle back to lower-right. You choose the square and the pattern; the die chooses the corner.
    2. Turning is always clockwise. A 4 turns a piece full circle, so it ends where it began; that still counts as your action.
    3. A 3/4 circle can only form round a corner shared by four squares.
    4. The pieces of a completed circle go back to their owners' hands and can be played again.
    5. A remove does not use the die. You cover any piece, your own or another player's, with a piece of the same pattern from your hand, and both come off. The covered piece goes back to its owner and yours goes back to you. Nobody scores. It uses one of your removes.
    6. The 10-point penalty falls on whoever's move leaves the board empty, whether by a circle or a remove; a circle's points are added first. Scores can go below zero.
    7. The win is checked after every move: the first to have completed a circle in all four patterns wins at once. Points still count: they decide the winner when a quiet round ends the game.
    8. The timer is on, as the rules ask; the table can turn it off before starting. With it on, the 20 seconds start when a person's turn starts, die roll included. If they run out, the turn passes with no action. Computer players are not timed.
    9. Every turn has a legal action, so there is no rule for passing: when the board holds a piece it can be turned, and when it is empty every piece is back in hand and can be added.
    10. The players can end a game together before anyone wins: each person playing presses End game for their own colour, and the game ends when the last of them does. Nobody wins, and the scores stand. Computer players are not asked.
    11. Hands are open, as on a table: everyone can see everyone's pieces.
    12. The coin toss picks the first player at random; play then goes round in seat order: Blue, Yellow, Graphite, White.
